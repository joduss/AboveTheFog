# Fast close-zoom panning across Switzerland

## What needs fixing

The last local production bundle ships the Fribourg and Zermatt pilot. An earlier desktop benchmark with empty browser storage took 3.50–6.03 s to settle detailed elevation on a direct Zurich visit. That benchmark used local app assets and live upstream elevation. The current source defaults to a different nationwide path. Its z7–8 overview exists and its z9–12 packs total 79.6 MB. There are no z13–16 indexes or packs in this checkout. Running the production asset verifier stops at the missing z13 index. The app displays through z19 by enlarging its finest terrain level. [Previous measurements](tile-loading-plan.md) · [National loader](../src/terrain/national.ts) · [Build verifier](../scripts/verify_national_assets.mjs)

The WebGL layer already compares a terrain sample with the selected altitude. The national loader fetches an entire packet before it can decode one member. At close zoom the planned 8 × 8 grouping can bring in 64 tiles for a small newly exposed strip. The national navigation branch currently skips both explicit preview loading and look-ahead. Loaded parents can help when already resident. Cold entries and crossings into a new packet are the cases to optimize. [Map navigation and rendering](../src/terrainMap.ts) · [Packet writer](../scripts/national/pyramid.py)

## First milestone

Before the countrywide build, take one continuous z16–19 pan through a former pilot boundary and across several packet boundaries. Use the same source samples and repeatable pointer trace for every configuration. Record cold, HTTP-warm and decoded-resident results. Use the first correct fine-fog pixel on the newly exposed strip as the primary latency measurement. Record the entire strip and viewport completion as well so optimizing one pixel cannot hide stalls elsewhere.

An exploratory nine-tile raw-source benchmark finished in 6.74 s with two workers and 4.69 s with four. First tile processing took 1.73 s and 2.46 s respectively, excluding worker startup. Upstream catalogue caches warmed between runs. These figures motivate measuring first paint and total completion separately. The production worker count for decoding prepared data needs its own experiment. [Worker benchmark and caveats](performance-investigation.md)

## Implementation order

1. **Establish the baseline and byte budget.** Extend the existing browser pan test to time the first painted fog on the newly exposed edge, its first correct fine-detail frame, the first legible basemap frame and the first frame with both. Time from the frame where the edge enters the viewport. For jumps, time from the navigation event. Trace request queue, payload bytes, decode, texture upload and frame times independently. Run on flat terrain, steep terrain and packet boundaries, including places outside the pilot. With the pilot build and national source, record the build identity and dataset version for each run.
2. **Choose a Pages-sized representation before generating the country.** Use existing source squares from the Plateau, Alps and border to compare the current Web Mercator pyramid with a version that stores the finest detail once, near the native 4 m spacing, plus cheaper overviews. One candidate stores the original LV95 band grid in compact blocks with precomputed placement in the map projection. Validate placement, seams and tap results against the source grid before adopting it. For close-zoom delivery, benchmark one tile per GET and independent 2 × 2 packets against the current 8 × 8 design. Include a pan that first touches only one tile in a new packet. Record total bytes fetched alongside the bytes needed for visible tiles. Every candidate must work with ordinary static GETs. Choose by cold edge-paint latency and total bytes, including indexes. At 20 Mbit/s even a 0.65 MB packet takes about 260 ms in payload time alone. Keep the map interactive through z19 and preserve the 4 m source grid, 10 m band semantics, nearest-neighbour display, equality staying clear and explicit NoData. Compare actual classifications at close zoom, especially where a ridge or valley is only a few cells wide.
3. **Finish one nationwide release.** Resume from the checksum-pinned swissALTI3D inventory and existing prepared squares. Reuse the existing parallel source-square preparation and packet writer. Once the shared source grid is verified and read-only, split delivery generation into disjoint zoom and packet-coordinate ranges. Each job writes its own hashed packets and a small result manifest. A single final job merges those manifests, verifies that every scheduled range completed exactly once, and publishes the index last. A failed range can resume from its verified outputs. Bound total preparation concurrency by memory, disk throughput and upstream download capacity. Keep pilot/vector and live GeoTIFF comparisons available to developers separately from the production bundle. Block release on missing assets. A normal pan or threshold change must issue zero elevation catalogue or source TIFF requests.
4. **Keep the fog visible during the gesture.** Display resident terrain immediately. On cold entry fetch a small covering parent and visible detail concurrently. Draw the best valid parent under each pending child, swap only when its texture is ready, and avoid double dimming. Mark approximate coverage during refinement and preserve amber for known NoData or failure. Update priorities during the drag as the viewport crosses tile boundaries. Fetch visible imagery and elevation concurrently. Start with visible terrain that has no valid parent, then visible fine detail, then one strip ahead in the direction of travel. Keep separate limits for network requests and decode work. Start decoding each visible tile as soon as its payload arrives and submit its texture for the next frame, even if neighbouring tiles are still downloading. Share requests and decodes by dataset version and tile key. On reversal, promote newly visible work, discard stale queued prefetches, and keep any active request that still has a visible consumer. Store versioned delivered bytes in the browser cache and bound decoded memory independently. Profile the per-frame availability polygon scan and the Float32 tile expansion on a phone. Benchmark the existing decoder first, then one and two decode workers with transferable buffers. Hold packet layout and request concurrency fixed for that comparison. Separately sweep simultaneous elevation requests over 2, 4 and 6 while the basemap is active. ## Phone memory budget

Each 256 × 256 terrain tile inflates to 128 KiB of signed 16-bit bands and expands to 256 KiB of Float32 samples. During conversion those two arrays alone occupy 384 KiB per active decode. The national compressed-packet cache retains up to 12 MiB. Its current size check happens after the entire response is buffered, and several requests can be active. The map also holds strong references to as many as 256 loaded tiles, potentially retaining 64 MiB of decoded samples through that map alone. The WebGL layer has a separate 128-entry texture cache. References to the same CPU buffer count only once. Queued CacheStorage writes retain tile results until written and copy the samples during the write. The 192-entry persistent cache represents about 48 MiB of stored payload. Actual peak RAM, browser overhead and GPU allocation still require device measurements. [Packet loader](../src/terrain/national.ts) · [Tile retention](../src/terrainMap.ts) · [Band conversion](../src/terrain/bands.ts) · [Persistent cache](../src/terrain/tileCache.ts)

Use **64 MiB as the initial test budget for app-managed terrain buffers in CPU memory**. Start with 32 MiB for decoded tiles, 12 MiB for retained compressed data and 20 MiB for in-flight fetch, decode, upload staging and persistence buffers combined. Track GPU texture estimates, basemap imagery and the tab's measured memory footprint separately. Reconcile shared buffers by identity and include worker-owned buffers. Tune these starting allocations on the test phone.

- Reserve bytes before admitting a download or decode, using verified packet lengths in the release manifest and the fixed decoded tile size. Enforce the read limit during streaming and release reservations on success, cancellation and failure. Evicted packets still used by decoders stay charged until the final consumer releases them.
- Tie coverage metadata to renderer residency so keeping a tile key cannot pin a decoded array after eviction. Pin visible tiles and covering parents during refinement. When short of headroom, drop look-ahead, evict offscreen tiles, and reduce simultaneous decodes. Defer fine detail behind an already visible parent when necessary. Bound the persistence queue by bytes and skip optional writes when storage is slow.
- On a physical phone, repeat a five-minute route across fresh terrain, reverse direction, zoom in and out, then revisit the starting view. Repeat with slow persistent storage. Record peak reserved and retained bytes, worker buffers, estimated GPU textures and browser process footprint where available, including after idle and disposal. Require the tracked CPU budget to hold at peak and the same-route footprint to plateau across repetitions, with the edge-paint targets still met. Record any unavailable GPU or process measurement explicitly.

## Proposed release criteria

Measure p95 on a physical midrange phone and a desktop at 20 Mbit/s down and 40 ms RTT, with at least 30 repetitions per tested route and cache state. Record cold, HTTP-warm and decoded-resident results separately. Include slow drags, sustained fast drags, reversals, fresh deep links and a route crossing a former pilot boundary at z16–19.

| Observable outcome | Proposed target |
| --- | --- |
| Newly exposed fog that was already decoded or prefetched | Visible within 50 ms of exposure |
| Newly exposed uncached detail during a pan | Correct fine fog at the sampled edge within 500 ms, with a resident parent visible by the next rendered frame where available |
| End of a pan into new terrain | Full visible terrain at requested detail within 1 s of release |
| Direct cold entry | Centre shows both legible imagery and valid approximate or finer fog within 500 ms |
| Altitude change on resident terrain | Correct fog within 50 ms and zero terrain transfers |
| Map motion | p95 frame interval at most 33 ms, zero flashes to amber over known valid resident terrain |

Report the basemap and terrain times alongside their combined time. Check rendered pixels over known threshold crossings and NoData as well as request diagnostics. A settled loader or a uniformly transparent frame alone cannot establish that the right fog was painted. Repeat on the deployed site before claiming near-instant panning. These are acceptance targets. Hosted and physical-phone measurements remain outstanding.
